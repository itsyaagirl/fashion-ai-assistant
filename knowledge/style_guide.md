# Accessory Styling Guide

## Neckline and necklaces
Match the necklace shape to the neckline. A V-neck pairs best with a pendant or a V-shaped chain that echoes the line. A crew neck or high neck suits a longer chain that sits below the collar, or no necklace with statement earrings instead. A strapless or off-shoulder top leaves room for a choker or a short collar necklace. A button-up shirt worn open works with a fine layered chain. A busy or embellished neckline usually needs no necklace at all.

## Earrings and face framing
Statement earrings work best when the neckline is simple and the hair is up or short. Small studs or thin hoops are the safe everyday choice and pair with almost any outfit. If wearing a statement necklace, keep earrings small so the two do not compete. Long drop earrings lengthen the look and suit evening and formal outfits.

## Bags and proportion
Balance the bag against the silhouette. Oversized or relaxed outfits look sharper with a structured or compact bag. Fitted, tailored outfits can take a softer slouchy tote. Crossbody and small shoulder bags suit casual, streetwear and travel looks because they keep hands free. Clutches and small top-handle bags belong with evening and formal outfits. A tote is the workhorse for office and smart-casual days.

## Belts
A belt defines the waist and can turn a loose dress, oversized blazer or high-waisted trousers into a more shaped outfit. Thin belts are elegant and suit dresses and tailoring. Wide belts make a bolder statement over dresses and long coats. Match belt hardware to the other metals in the outfit.

## Hats and hair accessories
Caps and beanies are casual and sporty; they pair with streetwear, denim and athleisure. Wide-brim and straw hats suit summer, boho and resort outfits. Berets add a polished, slightly retro touch to coats and knitwear. Claw clips, scrunchies and headbands are low-effort finishing touches that work with casual and minimalist looks.

## Scarves
A silk or satin scarf adds colour and polish; tie it at the neck, on a bag handle or in the hair. Chunky knit scarves belong with winter coats and knitwear. A scarf is the easiest way to add one accent colour to a neutral outfit.

## Sunglasses
Sunglasses are a year-round finishing piece for daytime outfits. Oversized frames read glamorous and luxe; small oval or rectangular frames read trend-led and streetwear; classic aviator and wayfarer shapes go with almost everything.

## Colour rules
Neutral outfits (black, white, grey, beige, denim, navy) can take accessories in any colour; one bright accessory is an easy way to lift them. With a bold or patterned outfit, choose neutral or metallic accessories so the outfit stays the focus. Monochrome looks (one colour family head to toe) feel modern and intentional. Complementary colours (opposites on the colour wheel, such as blue and orange) are bold and work best in small doses, such as a bag or earrings. Limit an outfit to about three main colours including accessories.

## Metals
Gold reads warm and pairs well with warm colours such as beige, brown, olive, cream, red and orange. Silver reads cool and pairs well with black, white, grey, blue and pastel tones. Mixing metals is acceptable when it looks deliberate, for example a two-tone watch or layered chains with both. Keep the bag hardware, belt buckle and jewellery in the same metal for a polished look.

## Occasion guide
Office and business: structured tote, simple watch, small studs, thin belt. Casual weekend: crossbody bag, cap or claw clip, sunglasses, simple hoops. Evening and party: clutch or mini bag, statement earrings or necklace, metallic accents. Wedding guest: elegant drop earrings, small clutch, delicate bracelet. Festival and summer: straw hat, layered necklaces, beaded or shell jewellery, small crossbody. Gym and athleisure: cap, sport bag or backpack, minimal jewellery.

## Season guide
Spring and summer favour lighter materials such as straw, raffia, canvas, shells, pearls and bright or pastel colours. Autumn and winter favour leather, suede, wool, knit scarves, beanies, gloves and deeper colours such as burgundy, brown and forest green.

## Persona: minimalist
The minimalist chooses few, high-quality pieces in neutral colours and clean shapes: a leather tote, a thin gold or silver chain, small studs, a plain watch. Avoids loud prints, logos and many accessories at once. One accessory focus per outfit.

## Persona: streetwear
The streetwear creator chooses bold, functional and trend-led pieces: caps, beanies, bucket hats, chunky chains, crossbody and sling bags, sporty sunglasses. Mixing high and low and following what is viral this week matters more than formality.

## Persona: luxe
The luxe stylist chooses polished, investment-looking pieces: structured leather bags, gold jewellery, pearls, silk scarves, sleek belts and oversized sunglasses. Everything should look intentional and refined.

## Persona: boho
The boho influencer chooses relaxed, earthy and handmade-looking pieces: fringe and woven bags, straw hats, beaded and shell jewellery, layered necklaces, warm browns and natural textures.

## How many accessories
Two to four accessories is the usual sweet spot: for example a bag, one jewellery focus and one practical piece such as sunglasses or a belt. If one accessory is a statement, keep the others quiet.
